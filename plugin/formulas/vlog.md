# Vlog formula

For videos about people and a day, a week or a trip. Derived from David Dobrik's channel (all 569 videos, 24 thumbnails, and a 9-video breakdown), measured 2026-11-09, and written so it works without celebrities or giveaways. The reasoning it shares with the other formats is in `formulas { id: "algorithm" }`.

Numbers marked "×" compare median views inside his channel; they show what goes with more views, not proof of cause: old videos have had longer to collect views, and big ideas get both the bold title and the views.

## The question this format sells
**"How will this person react?"** The answer is a face. Everything else sets up that face and gets out of its way.

## 1. The idea
- **A gap:** a person the audience knows, put in a moment outside their normal life: a surprise, a meeting, a confession, a dare.
- **Something at risk between people:** a secret, a feeling, pride. Titles with CAUGHT, CONFESSED, CONFRONTED: ×1.62, his strongest pattern.
- **The reaction must be real.** Plan the setup, never the response.
- Low-budget versions: a small surprise for someone who doesn't expect it, a first meeting, a bet between friends, telling someone something they don't know.

## 2. The cast
- A small group that returns every video, each with one clear trait.
- Viewers come back for the people, so introduce a newcomer with a name label and one line.
- Don't make anyone look worse than they were.

## 3. Title
- **Classic:** capitals, about 6 words, ending "!!".
- **Hide who or what:** "SHE", "HE", "THIS": ×1.17, and seven of his top 12.
- **A bracket tag** for the reaction, "(FREAKOUT)", "(CAUGHT)": ×1.33.
- "SURPRISING…" alone adds nothing (×1.00); it needs a name or a stake.
- **Calmer version:** sentence case, first person, 3–6 words ("This was a bad idea").

## 4. Thumbnail
- Faces, not things: 2–4 people, mouths open, laughing or shocked.
- Your own face large on one side; the person or thing causing the reaction on the other.
- No text. A red circle at most.
- Bright. Staging it separately is fine, as long as the moment is in the video.

## 5. The vlog, in order
| Where | What |
|---|---|
| 0–12 s | A standalone gag. It doesn't preview the main bit. |
| ~12 s | A 2-second title card: name, vlog number, date. Same every time. |
| To ~1 min | The premise, said fast to camera (200+ words a minute). |
| The middle | The main bit: fast build-up, then the reveal. |
| The reveal | One fixed camera, held 50–90 seconds. Don't cut away from the face. |
| After it | A music-led stretch with almost no talking. |
| Near the end | Plug cards. |
| Last 5–20 s | One more gag. Never end on a card. |

## 6. Keeping people through it
- **Two speeds.** 15–25 cuts a minute everywhere except the reveal, which is slow.
- **Cut out on the laugh.** A real laugh tells the viewer the joke landed.
- **Cut in on the line before the joke;** drop the setup talk.
- **Each bit sets up and pays off in under a minute.**
- **One song per bit,** dropped for the punchline. Use licensed library music.
- **A fixed, short length.** His 4:20 videos have 2.4× the views of his other lengths. Pick a length and keep it.

## 7. What matters most (my estimate)
Nobody outside YouTube can measure each part's true share. These shares are an estimate, not a measurement.

| Part | Share |
|---|---|
| The idea: a real moment with something at risk between people | 30% |
| Title and thumbnail | 20% |
| The cast and their real reactions | 20% |
| The edit: pace, cutting on laughs, the held reveal | 15% |
| The ritual: fixed length, title card, end gag, schedule | 10% |
| Music | 5% |

## Checklist before publishing
- [ ] The title hides who or what, and promises one moment.
- [ ] The thumbnail is faces reacting, with no words.
- [ ] It opens on a gag within seconds.
- [ ] The premise is clear by one minute.
- [ ] The reveal is held on one camera.
- [ ] Nothing slow except the reveal.
- [ ] It ends on a gag, not a card.
- [ ] Everyone shown agreed to be in it.

### Titles
- Capitals on 409 of 569; "!!" on 538.
- Classic length: 2–11 words, 6 typical, about 38 characters.
- No apostrophes in many ("WE CANT BELIEVE THIS HAPPENED!!").
- Bracket tag on 99.
- Hiding who or what ("SHE", "HE", "HER", "THIS"): 207 of 457 classic titles.
- Drama words (CAUGHT, CONFESSED, CONFRONTED, EXPOSED, SECRET): ×1.62, the strongest pattern found. Romance words ×1.08; money or a car ×1.15.
- "SURPRISING…" is his most used word (104 titles); with a celebrity's name it reaches the top 12 twice.
- New style: sentence case, 3–6 words, first person, no "!!" ("Our first kiss", "This was a bad idea"); 3.5–4.7M views each.

### Inside the video (9 videos broken down)
- Cold open 8–15 s; title card about 2 s at 0:10–0:15, white monospace on black.
- Premise 35–50 s at 200–236 words a minute; 55–74% of all words fall in the first minute.
- Headline bit from about 1:05 to 3:55.
- Group scenes: 14–23+ shot changes a minute, shots of 1–3 s.
- Reveal: one fixed camera, 50–90 s, about 2 changes a minute.
- Back half: music-led, 20+ cuts a minute, almost no talking.
- Plug cards at about 3:54–4:14, then a 4–20 s tag.
- Music: one licensed song per bit; 6–10 tracks in a long vlog; one signature outro song.
- On-screen text: bold lowercase meme captions; now yellow subtitles on chosen lines and yellow name labels with a joke descriptor; time cards ("EARLIER").
- Sponsor: classic, a 35 s read after the title card or the brand written into the stunt; now one 30–45 s read 21–70% of the way in.
- Cadence: 2–3 a week at the peak; every other week now.
- Editing time: 12–15 hours for one 4:20 vlog from about 5 hours of footage.
- Description: schedule line, sponsor code, plugs, socials, "other people in the video", credits.
- Shorts: 23–43 s, hook in the first frame, 1–2-word captions, end mid-beat.

### Not measured
Slow motion and sound effects, the fastest cut rates, whether thumbnails are tested against each other, and click or retention rates.
